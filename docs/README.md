How OctoAcme manages projects
OctoAcme’s project lifecycle starts with initiation, where the team confirms the business need, success measures, stakeholders, and whether the work should proceed. During planning, the team turns approved work into a prioritized backlog, defines acceptance criteria and the Definition of Done, estimates work, and identifies milestones, risks, and dependencies.

During execution, the team tracks work on a project board, communicates progress and blockers, and delivers small increments through reviewed pull requests. The team uses tests, CI checks, security scanning, and QA to support quality. For release, the team checks that acceptance criteria and release requirements are met, deploys, verifies the result, and communicates the release. After a sprint, release, or milestone, the team holds a retrospective and tracks follow-up improvements.

The Project Manager (PM) coordinates plans, risks, schedules, and communication. The Product Manager defines outcomes, prioritizes work, and measures success. Developers build and test changes; QA/testing helps validate acceptance criteria and quality; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. The team uses standups and delivery syncs to share progress and raise blockers, regular PM and Product Manager alignment, and stakeholder updates at agreed intervals.

Risks and dependencies are recorded, assigned owners, given mitigation plans, and reviewed regularly. Blockers are raised first with the team, then escalated through the PM and Product Lead to the sponsor when needed. Quality practices include unit and integration testing where appropriate, CI tests and linting, security scanning, and smoke tests for critical flows before release.
